# Card artwork provenance

Evara uses the **78-card Rider–Waite–Smith deck**, illustrated by **Pamela Colman Smith** under the direction of **Arthur Edward Waite**, first published in 1909. The imagery is historical Rider–Waite–Smith artwork; it is not Gilded Tarot artwork.

The interpretations in English and Vietnamese are newly written, constructive reflections informed by the traditional imagery. Historical reference: [A. E. Waite, The Pictorial Key to the Tarot (1910), Part III §2](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Pictorial_Key_to_the_Tarot/Part_3). Historical fortune-telling claims are not presented as factual predictions.

## Major Arcana, cards 1–22

The 22 existing files are unmodified copies of the 720px JPG scans distributed by [mixvlad/TarotCards](https://github.com/mixvlad/TarotCards), whose project identifies the deck as public domain.

Source directory: https://github.com/mixvlad/TarotCards/tree/main/tarot/rider-waite/720px

Local filenames correspond to source `00_Fool.jpg` through `21_World.jpg`, renamed to card-name slugs. Original internal IDs 0–21 are unchanged so previously saved readings remain valid.

## Minor Arcana, cards 23–78

All 56 files below are unmodified 500px JPEG thumbnails supplied by Wikimedia Commons from scans of the historical “Pam-A” deck. Each file's Commons image-info record was checked for **Public domain** licensing and for the correct card title before download on 7 October 2026. The source pages identify the historical artist as Pamela Colman Smith and the original artwork as public domain, with the [Public Domain Mark](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/). Recent Commons uploads are scans of that historical work.

No local image editing, recoloring, or generated replacement art was applied. The app vendors these files so card viewing does not contact third-party image hosts.
